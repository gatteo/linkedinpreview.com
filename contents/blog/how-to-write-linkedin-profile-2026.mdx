Most people treat their LinkedIn profile like a static resume—a document that outlines where they’ve been and what they’ve done.

In 2026, if you want your profile to drive business, attract top-tier opportunities, or build your authority, you need to flip that mindset. Your LinkedIn profile shouldn't be a resume; it should be a **high-converting landing page for you.**

In this guide, I’ll show you how to write a LinkedIn profile that turns views into valuable professional connections.

## 1. The "Hook" - Your Headline

Your headline is the first thing people see. Stop using just your job title (e.g., "Marketing Manager").

**The Conversion Formula:**
[Role] | Helping [Target Audience] achieve [Desired Outcome] through [Your Unique Method/Skill]

## 2. The Narrative - Your "About" Section

Your About section is not a place to repeat your work experience. It is where you tell your story.

- **The Opening**: State clearly what problem you solve and for whom.
- **The Journey**: Briefly explain _why_ you do what you do. This humanizes your brand.
- **The Value**: Use bullet points (using a [LinkedIn content formatter](/blog/linkedin-content-formatter-guide)) to list your core services or the specific outcomes you've delivered for past clients/employers.

## 3. The Evidence - Experience & Featured Sections

Don’t just list responsibilities. List _accomplishments_.

- **Experience**: Use action verbs and include metrics. Instead of "Managed a team," write "Led a team of 10 to a 20% increase in productivity over 6 months."
- **Featured**: This is your showcase. Link to your best LinkedIn posts, case studies, or a landing page for your newsletter. (Read more in our [Featured Section guide](/blog/linkedin-featured-section))
