## Can You Add or Change Images After Publishing?

**No.** Once a post is live, you **cannot** add, replace, or remove attached images, videos, or documents.

LinkedIn does not provide an "Edit Media" option for posts. The media you attach at the moment of publication becomes a permanent part of the post.

## What CAN You Edit?

While you are stuck with your media choice, you can still edit:

- **Text**: Fix typos, reformat your content (using a [LinkedIn formatting tool](/blog/linkedin-post-formatter)), or update your Call to Action.
- **Hashtags**: Add or remove hashtags to optimize for search visibility.
- **Mentions**: Tag or untag users and company pages.

## The "Delete and Repost" Decision

Since you cannot change the media, your only option for a broken image is to **Delete and Repost**. But should you?

### When to Delete and Repost:

- **The Media is Broken**: If your video won't play, your PDF is corrupt, or your image is blurry/unreadable, the post is not providing value. Delete and repost.
- **The Visual is Crucial**: If the post _relies_ on the visual (e.g., a carousel where the cover slide is the primary hook), and the visual is wrong, you must repost.

### When to Keep the Post:

- **The Post Has Significant Engagement**: If your post has already gathered dozens of comments and likes, **do not delete it**. The loss of engagement is a much bigger penalty than a minor visual error. In this case, acknowledge the error in the comments, and move on.

## How to Minimize Media Errors (Workflow)

1.  **Draft and Format**: Use a tool like [linkedinpreview.com](https://linkedinpreview.com/#tool) to format your text (bold/italics/lists) _before_ you open LinkedIn.
2.  **Verify Media Quality**: Before attaching, double-check that your images are the correct resolution (e.g., 1200x630 px for OG) and your carousel PDF is exported correctly.
3.  **Use a Preview Tool**: Always preview your post on a dedicated tool to see how your media sits within the layout.
4.  **Final Proofread**: Read the entire post for clarity and tone, including the media captions.

## Final Thoughts

The LinkedIn editor is unforgiving when it comes to media, but that just means a proactive workflow pays off. Use preview tools to double-check your work _before_ you hit publish, and treat the "delete" button as a last resort.
