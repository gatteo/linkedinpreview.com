## What Is LinkedIn Dwell Time?

Dwell time is the amount of time a LinkedIn user spends looking at your post. LinkedIn tracks two distinct types:

**On-feed dwell time** is measured while a user scrolls through their feed. If at least 50% of your post is visible on screen, LinkedIn starts counting. Even a few seconds here registers as a signal.

**After-click dwell time** starts when someone taps "see more" to expand your post. This is the stronger signal because it proves active interest - the reader chose to read more.

Both types feed into LinkedIn's content distribution algorithm. The longer someone dwells on your content, the more LinkedIn interprets that as a quality signal and shows the post to additional users.

Think of it like a feedback loop: more dwell time leads to broader distribution, which leads to more impressions, which creates more opportunities for engagement.

## Why Dwell Time Matters More Than Likes in 2026

LinkedIn made a deliberate shift toward dwell time because likes were easy to game. Engagement pods, reciprocal liking, and "agree?" posts inflated like counts without reflecting genuine content quality.

Here is what the data shows:

- **A post someone reads for 30+ seconds outperforms a post with 50 quick likes.** The algorithm treats sustained attention as a stronger quality signal than passive engagement.
- **Posts with 61+ seconds of average dwell time achieve a 15.6% engagement rate** - roughly 15x higher than posts people scroll past in under 5 seconds.
- **Dwell time affects both first-hour and extended distribution.** LinkedIn initially shows your post to a small test audience. If that audience dwells, the post gets pushed wider. High dwell time in the first 60-90 minutes is critical.

This does not mean likes are worthless. Likes, comments, and shares still contribute to the algorithm. But dwell time is now the foundational signal that determines whether your post even gets the chance to collect those engagements.

## How LinkedIn Measures Dwell Time (The Technical Side)

LinkedIn does not publish exact dwell time thresholds, but testing and LinkedIn's own engineering blog posts have revealed the mechanics:

**Viewport tracking.** LinkedIn monitors whether your post is visible in the user's viewport. The timer starts when at least 50% of the post is on screen. Fast scrolling does not count - there is a minimum threshold before a view registers.

**Click-to-expand tracking.** When someone clicks "see more," LinkedIn logs the timestamp. When they scroll away or close the post, it logs the end time. The delta is your after-click dwell time.

**Weighted scoring.** After-click dwell time carries more weight than on-feed dwell time. A user who expands your post and reads for 45 seconds sends a much stronger signal than someone who pauses for 3 seconds while scrolling.

**Negative signals.** If a user sees your post and immediately scrolls past (under 1-2 seconds of visibility), that counts as a negative signal. It tells LinkedIn the headline or opening did not catch their attention - and repeated negative signals suppress the post.

## 9 Tactics to Increase Your LinkedIn Dwell Time

### 1. Write a Hook That Forces the "See More" Click

The most critical dwell time event is the "see more" click. Every expanded view is a strong intent signal that tells LinkedIn your content is worth distributing further.

Your first 2-3 lines must create enough curiosity or promise enough value that the reader clicks to read the rest. Effective patterns:

- **Counterintuitive claim:** "Most LinkedIn advice about posting frequency is wrong. Here is what actually matters."
- **Specific data point:** "I analyzed 200 LinkedIn posts last month. The ones that got the most reach had one thing in common."
- **Direct challenge:** "Stop writing LinkedIn posts that start with 'I'm excited to announce.'"
- **Incomplete story:** "Last Tuesday a prospect sent me a message that changed how I think about cold outreach."

### 2. Hit the Sweet Spot on Post Length

Too short and there is nothing to dwell on. Too long and people bounce before finishing.

Research from multiple LinkedIn content studies converges on a range: **150-300 words is ideal for text posts** that maximize both "see more" clicks and read-through completion. Posts in this range force the "see more" expansion while staying short enough that most readers finish.

For pillar-style posts where you are teaching a framework or sharing detailed analysis, **300-500 words** works if the content is scannable and well-formatted.

Going beyond 500 words in a text post risks drop-off unless the topic is highly compelling. If you have that much to say, a document/carousel post handles long-form content better because each slide resets the reader's attention.

For detailed guidance on length by post type, check our [LinkedIn post length guide](/blog/linkedin-post-length-guide-2026).

### 3. Format for Readability, Not Just Aesthetics

Formatting directly affects dwell time because it controls reading speed and comprehension. Walls of text make people scroll past. Structured, scannable content keeps them reading.

Key formatting rules for dwell time:

- **One sentence per line, maximum two.** Line breaks create white space that makes content feel less dense.
- **Bold key phrases** so skimmers can grasp the main points and then decide to read the full text.
- **Use numbered lists and bullet points** for any sequence or set of items.
- **Keep paragraphs to 1-3 sentences.** LinkedIn's narrow mobile column makes long paragraphs look overwhelming.

Good formatting is invisible - readers just feel like the post is "easy to read." Bad formatting creates friction that kills dwell time. See our [LinkedIn posting best practices](/blog/linkedin-posting-best-practices) for more on structuring your content.

### 4. Use the 1-3-1 Post Structure

The [1-3-1 framework](/blog/linkedin-post-format-1-3-1-structure) is purpose-built for dwell time:

- **1 hook line** (drives the "see more" click)
- **3 body sections** (delivers the value, keeps reader engaged)
- **1 closing line** (call to action or memorable takeaway)

This structure works because it gives readers a clear beginning, middle, and end. They know roughly how much content to expect, which reduces the urge to scroll away. Each of the three body sections acts as a mini reset point that pulls the reader forward.

### 5. Lead With Specifics, Not Generalities

Generic statements get skimmed. Specific details force the brain to slow down and process.

Compare these two openers:

**Low dwell time:** "Content strategy is important for growth on LinkedIn."

**High dwell time:** "I posted 47 times on LinkedIn last quarter. 3 posts generated 80% of my profile visits. Here is what those 3 had in common."

Numbers, names, dates, and concrete examples create what psychologists call "processing fluency interrupts." The brain cannot skim past specific data the way it skims past platitudes. Every time you include a concrete detail, you add seconds to your dwell time.

### 6. Create Document and Carousel Posts

Document posts (uploaded as PDFs) generate 2-3x more dwell time than plain text posts. Each slide is a separate dwell event, so a 10-slide carousel can accumulate 10 individual attention moments.

Carousel posts work because:

- **They gamify reading.** Swiping through slides feels interactive.
- **Each slide resets attention.** Even if a reader's focus drifts, the next slide pulls them back.
- **They handle long-form content better than text.** A 1000-word idea split across 10 slides feels shorter than a 1000-word wall of text.

### 7. Tell Stories With Tension

Narratives create a psychological pull called the "curiosity gap." When you introduce a problem or conflict early in a post, readers stay to find out how it resolves.

A simple story structure for LinkedIn:

1. **Setup** (1-2 lines): The situation before.
2. **Conflict** (2-3 lines): What went wrong or what was unexpected.
3. **Resolution** (2-3 lines): What you learned or how you solved it.
4. **Takeaway** (1 line): The lesson the reader can apply.

Stories with tension hold attention because the brain's reward system activates in anticipation of the resolution. This is why "I got fired" posts consistently outperform "5 tips for productivity" posts in dwell time - the conflict creates an emotional investment that keeps readers scrolling.

### 8. Add a Question or Prompt Mid-Post

Placing a question in the middle of your post (not just at the end) creates a micro-pause. The reader stops to consider their own answer before continuing.

Examples:

- "How many of you have experienced this exact scenario?"
- "Take a second and think about the last time this happened to you."
- "Here is the part most people get wrong. Can you guess what it is?"

These prompts add 5-15 seconds of dwell time each because they shift the reader from passive consumption to active reflection.

### 9. Avoid Dwell Time Killers

Some content patterns actively destroy dwell time:

- **External links in the post body.** Clicking a link takes the user off LinkedIn. The algorithm deprioritizes posts with outbound URLs because they end the dwell session.
- **Engagement bait ("Agree?" / "Like if you..." / "Comment your answer").** LinkedIn's algorithm specifically detects and suppresses these patterns. They also signal low-quality content that savvy readers scroll past.
- **Clickbait hooks with no payoff.** If your hook promises something the body does not deliver, readers bounce immediately after clicking "see more." That short after-click dwell time is worse than no click at all because LinkedIn interprets it as misleading content.
- **Posting screenshots of text.** Images of text are hard to read on mobile, and users scroll past content that requires zooming or squinting.

## How to Measure Your Dwell Time Performance

LinkedIn does not show raw dwell time numbers in creator analytics. But you can infer dwell time performance from these proxy metrics:

**Impressions-to-engagement ratio.** If your impressions are high but engagement is low, people are seeing your post but not dwelling long enough to interact. Your hook or formatting needs work.

**"See more" click rate.** LinkedIn shows this in detailed post analytics. A healthy "see more" rate is above 3%. Below that, your opening lines are not compelling enough to drive after-click dwell time.

**Saves.** A save is one of the strongest dwell time proxy signals. People only save content they have actually read and found valuable enough to revisit.

**Comments that reference specific parts of your post.** If comments quote or react to points from the middle or end of your post, readers are making it all the way through. If all comments are generic ("Great post!"), people may only be reading the hook.

Track these metrics over 10-20 posts to identify patterns. You will start to see which post formats, lengths, and topics generate the deepest engagement for your specific audience.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does video content get more dwell time than text posts?

Native LinkedIn video - especially vertical, 60-90 second clips - can generate strong dwell time because the platform auto-plays video as you scroll. However, a well-formatted text post with a strong hook often outperforms video in after-click dwell time. The best approach depends on your content style and audience preferences.

### Does editing a post reset its dwell time metrics?

No. LinkedIn tracks dwell time from the moment of first publication. Editing your post does not reset these metrics, but it also does not give you a fresh distribution cycle. Your post keeps whatever momentum (or lack thereof) it already had.

### Do hashtags affect dwell time?

Hashtags do not directly affect dwell time but they influence who sees your post in the first place. Using 3-5 relevant hashtags helps your post reach people interested in the topic, who are more likely to stop and read. Irrelevant or excessive hashtags attract the wrong audience, leading to fast scroll-pasts and negative dwell signals.

### Is dwell time the same across desktop and mobile?

LinkedIn tracks dwell time on both platforms, but mobile users tend to scroll faster. This is why formatting matters even more on mobile - short paragraphs, line breaks, and bold text slow down the mobile scroll and increase dwell time. [Preview your posts](/blog/linkedin-hook-preview) on mobile before publishing to check readability.

### Can I see who dwelled on my post?

No. LinkedIn does not provide individual-level dwell time data. You can see who viewed your profile and who engaged with your post, but dwell time is an aggregate metric used internally by the algorithm. Focus on the proxy metrics mentioned above to gauge your performance.
